OBJECTIVES OF THE THESIS
The primary objective of this thesis is to examine the structural determinants of Risk Management Effectiveness in Hybrid Project Environments, with particular emphasis on the role of AI-Driven Strategic Risk Management. Hybrid project environments, characterized by the coexistence of Agile and Waterfall methodologies, introduce structural complexity, governance fragmentation, and dynamic risk interactions that challenge traditional risk management approaches. This research seeks to empirically investigate how strategic governance mechanisms and AI-driven analytical capabilities jointly influence effective risk outcomes.
The specific objectives of the thesis are as follows:
To examine the impact of Risk Process Alignment between Agile and Waterfall methodologies on Risk Management Effectiveness in hybrid project environments.
To analyse the influence of Risk Visibility Integration on Risk Management Effectiveness in hybrid projects.
To assess the effect of Timely Risk Escalation on Risk Management Effectiveness in hybrid project environments.
To evaluate the role of Governance Consistency in enhancing Risk Management Effectiveness in hybrid projects.
To examine the impact of AI-Driven Risk Analytics Capability on overall Risk Management Effectiveness in hybrid project environments using Structural Equation Modelling (SEM).

Abstract
The increasing adoption of hybrid project methodologies integrating Agile adaptability with Waterfall structure has introduced significant complexity into organizational risk governance. Traditional risk management approaches, primarily reliant on manual assessment and static control frameworks, often struggle to address the dynamic and fragmented nature of hybrid environments. Concurrently, Artificial Intelligence (AI) has emerged as a strategic enabler of predictive analytics, real-time monitoring, and data-driven decision support. However, limited empirical research has examined how AI capability interacts with structural governance mechanisms to influence overall risk management effectiveness in hybrid project settings. This study investigates the structural determinants of Risk Management Effectiveness in Hybrid Project Environments, with particular emphasis on AI-Driven Risk Analytics Capability. A quantitative research design was employed using primary data collected from 384 project professionals across diverse industries. Structural Equation Modelling (SEM) was utilized to examine the direct effects of five key dimensions: Risk Process Alignment, Risk Visibility Integration, Timely Risk Escalation, Governance Consistency, and AI-Driven Risk Analytics Capability. The findings reveal that Risk Process Alignment, Governance Consistency, and AI-Driven Risk Analytics Capability have statistically significant and positive effects on Risk Management Effectiveness. Among these, AI-Driven Risk Analytics Capability emerged as one of the strongest predictors, indicating that advanced analytical intelligence substantially enhances predictive foresight and decision quality in hybrid settings. In contrast, Risk Visibility Integration demonstrated only a marginal direct effect, while Timely Risk Escalation did not exhibit a statistically significant influence. These results suggest that structural coherence and technological capability exert greater impact on risk outcomes than operational transparency or escalation responsiveness alone. The study contributes to project management and risk governance literature by positioning AI capability as a strategic structural determinant rather than a peripheral technological tool. It advances hybrid project theory by demonstrating that effectiveness depends on deliberate integration of governance consistency and process alignment. Practically, the findings provide evidence-based guidance for managers seeking to strengthen risk governance in complex project ecosystems. The research concludes that sustainable risk performance in hybrid environments requires integrated structural alignment, governance stability, and AI-driven analytical intelligence.

CHAPTER-1
Introduction
The contemporary project landscape is undergoing a profound transformation driven by technological advancement, organizational globalization, and increasing environmental volatility. Projects today are no longer isolated, linear undertakings; instead, they are complex socio-technical systems embedded within dynamic organizational and external environments. This complexity has significantly increased project exposure to uncertainty, making risk an inherent and continuous feature across the project lifecycle rather than a discrete event addressed at predefined stages (Flyvbjerg, 2017; Williams et al., 2010).
Despite the centrality of risk in modern projects, traditional project risk management practices remain largely rooted in deterministic planning paradigms. These practices typically rely on static risk registers, periodic assessments, and subjective expert judgment, which often fail to capture evolving risk interactions, emergent threats, and non-linear causal relationships (Ward & Chapman, 2003; Aven, 2016). As project environments become more data-intensive and time-sensitive, such approaches increasingly struggle to provide timely, accurate, and actionable insights for decision-makers.
Artificial Intelligence (AI) has emerged as a critical enabler capable of addressing these limitations by fundamentally redefining how project risks are identified, assessed, and managed. Rather than serving as a standalone automation tool, AI represents a paradigm shift toward adaptive, learning-based, and evidence-driven risk governance. By leveraging machine learning, predictive analytics, and intelligent monitoring systems, AI enables continuous sensing of project conditions, early detection of weak risk signals, and dynamic updating of risk profiles in real time (Jordan & Mitchell, 2015; Raisch & Krakowski, 2021).
AI-driven analytical models are particularly effective in environments characterized by large volumes of heterogeneous data. Project data—ranging from schedules, budgets, and performance metrics to communication logs and stakeholder feedback—can be integrated and analyzed to uncover latent patterns and interdependencies that are beyond human cognitive capacity (Provost & Fawcett, 2013; Biesenthal et al., 2022). Predictive models built on such data can forecast schedule slippages, cost escalations, and performance degradation with greater accuracy than traditional rule-based methods, enabling earlier and more targeted risk mitigation interventions (Zhang et al., 2021; Shao et al., 2025).
From a decision-theoretic perspective, AI-based risk management systems address inherent limitations of human judgment, including bounded rationality, cognitive bias, and information overload (Simon, 1997; Kahneman, 2011). While human expertise remains essential for contextual interpretation and ethical judgment, AI enhances decision quality by providing consistent, scalable, and data-driven insights. This synergy between human and artificial intelligence has given rise to hybrid decision-making models that improve both the speed and robustness of risk-related decisions in complex projects (Raisch & Krakowski, 2021; Hanaysha et al., 2021).
The growing maturity of AI technologies has accelerated their adoption in large-scale and mission-critical projects. Organizations operating in sectors such as manufacturing, finance, aerospace, and public infrastructure increasingly embed AI-based systems within their project governance frameworks to enhance visibility, automate risk monitoring, and optimize response strategies (Bughin et al., 2018; Treacy et al., 2025). These developments indicate a shift from experimental adoption toward institutionalized use of AI in project risk management, signaling its strategic importance for organizational resilience and competitiveness.
At the same time, the integration of AI into project management raises important questions regarding governance, transparency, and managerial control. Effective use of AI requires alignment between technological capabilities, organizational processes, and decision authority structures. Without such alignment, AI systems risk becoming opaque tools that generate outputs without meaningful integration into managerial workflows (Marnewick et al., 2020; Dwivedi et al., 2023). Consequently, understanding how AI-enabled risk management operates within real project contexts has become a critical area of scholarly inquiry.
Background and Significance of Artificial Intelligence in IT Project Management
The relevance of AI is particularly pronounced in the context of IT project management, where uncertainty, complexity, and rapid change are defining characteristics. IT projects often involve evolving requirements, fast-paced technological innovation, and interdependent system architectures, making them highly susceptible to risks related to scope creep, integration failure, and resource misalignment (Sauer et al., 2007; Standish Group, 2020). Historical evidence consistently shows that a significant proportion of IT projects fail to meet their original objectives, underscoring the need for more advanced and adaptive risk management approaches.
Traditional IT project management frameworks emphasize upfront planning and periodic control mechanisms, which may be insufficient in environments where requirements and technologies evolve continuously. Artificial Intelligence introduces a fundamentally different approach by enabling continuous, real-time decision support across the entire project lifecycle. Through the application of machine learning, natural language processing (NLP), and advanced analytics, AI systems assist project managers in interpreting complex information streams and anticipating future project states with greater accuracy (Wamba et al., 2021; Siau & Wang, 2018).
One of the most significant contributions of AI to IT project management lies in its ability to integrate structured and unstructured data sources. Structured data, such as schedules, budgets, and defect metrics, can be combined with unstructured data, including emails, meeting transcripts, and issue reports, to generate a more comprehensive and nuanced understanding of project risk exposure (Provost & Fawcett, 2013; Biesenthal et al., 2022). This holistic data integration enables earlier detection of emerging risks and supports proactive rather than reactive management strategies.
AI systems further enhance IT project risk management through adaptive learning capabilities. As projects progress and new data become available, AI models can update their predictions and recommendations, maintaining relevance in dynamic environments. This continuous learning feature is particularly valuable in IT projects, where changing stakeholder expectations and technological dependencies can rapidly alter risk profiles (March, 1991; Zhang et al., 2021).
In addition to analytical improvements, AI has reshaped coordination and communication mechanisms within IT project teams. AI-enabled dashboards, intelligent assistants, and automated reporting tools facilitate real-time information sharing and improve transparency across geographically distributed teams (Fountaine et al., 2019). Enhanced visibility and communication are critical for managing interdependencies and ensuring timely escalation of risks in complex IT projects.
From a strategic and sustainability perspective, AI-enabled risk management contributes to long-term organizational resilience. Sustainable project outcomes depend not only on meeting immediate performance targets but also on managing risks that threaten long-term value creation, social responsibility, and resource efficiency (Elkington, 1997; United Nations, 2015). By improving risk anticipation, decision quality, and adaptive capacity, AI strengthens organizational ability to navigate uncertainty and supports more sustainable and responsible IT project execution (Aven & Renn, 2010)
Theoretical Background
Definition of Project Risk Management
Project risk management is a systematic and structured process that aims to identify, assess, and manage risks that may affect project results. It is a process that enables the measurement of risks and the development of strategies to mitigate risks and avoid undesirable outcomes (Faruk, 2025). (Kaplan & Garrick, 1981) describe risk as a product of the probability of occurrence of an event and its potential consequences. This is a quantitative method that enables organizations to manage risks and develop strategies to avoid disruptions.
An effective risk management process includes a number of key elements, such as risk identification, risk analysis, risk response planning, and risk monitoring. Risk identification is the process of identifying potential risks that may impede the success of a project, while risk analysis is the process of determining the risk potential of each risk situation. After the risks have been analysed, risk response plans can be developed, which may include risk avoidance, risk reduction, risk transfer, or risk acceptance.
AI technologies Applicable to Risk Management
The incorporation of artificial intelligence (AI) in risk management has revolutionized the way risks are identified, analysed and mitigated. AI-based risk management tools are more efficient, accurate, and scalable than their conventional counterparts, allowing organizations to effectively mitigate risks in real-time. AI-based risk assessment employs a range of advanced technologies to enhance predictive accuracy and automate risk mitigation plans (Dutta, D., & Bose, 2015). The primary AI technologies that can be leveraged for risk management are:
Machine Learning (ML) – Machine learning algorithms are used to analyse large datasets to detect patterns and predict risks, and then suggest ways to mitigate them. Machine learning models improve their accuracy levels by learning from new data, making them very effective in detecting anomalies, predicting system failures, and identifying vulnerabilities in critical infrastructure systems. 
Natural Language Processing (NLP) – NLP technologies help AI systems to derive meaningful insights from unstructured text sources like security updates, intelligence meetings, social media updates, and live communications. By analysing language patterns, NLP-based AI solutions can detect potential threats, point out misinformation campaigns, and offer warning signs for potential risks.
Deep Learning (DL) – Deep learning algorithms, especially neural networks, improve risk detection abilities by processing unstructured data, like images, videos, and sensor inputs. Deep learning models are commonly employed in domains such as biometric security, surveillance analytics, and automated anomaly detection in cybersecurity solutions (Wenzel, M., Krause, B., & Wagner, 2022).
Through the integration of these AI technologies, risk management frameworks can be made more adaptable, precise, and responsive. AI-based risk assessment tools are capable of handling large amounts of diverse data, thus minimizing the possibility of human error and allowing organizations to shift from a reactive to a proactive risk management strategy. The ability of AI to quickly analyse risk scenarios, simulate risks, and provide recommendations on the best risk mitigation strategies makes it a crucial component of risk governance frameworks.
Theoretical background of risk management offers the required context to appreciate the role of AI in improving risk identification, mitigation, and response strategies. Conventional risk management strategies, although helpful, have become less effective in dealing with the dynamic risks that critical infrastructure and national security projects are exposed to. The adoption of AI technologies such as ML, NLP, and DL has brought about a paradigm shift in risk management strategies, enabling real-time analysis of threats and automated decision support. As AI technology evolves, its application in risk management will become more prominent, leading to improvements in resilience, efficiency, and overall security in high-risk sectors.
Problem Statement
The rapid evolution of project environments particularly the rise of hybrid methodologies combining Agile flexibility with Waterfall structure has significantly altered the nature of risk management. While traditional project risk management literature has extensively examined structured risk identification, qualitative and quantitative assessment methods, and governance frameworks, most studies are grounded in conventional predictive environments. These studies emphasize documentation, control mechanisms, and hierarchical escalation structures but do not adequately address the procedural complexity introduced when adaptive and predictive methodologies coexist within the same project ecosystem.
In parallel, a growing body of research has explored the application of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in project management and risk governance. These studies primarily focus on machine learning for cost prediction, predictive analytics for schedule overruns, anomaly detection in IT systems, and automation of risk monitoring dashboards. Although this body of literature demonstrates the technological potential of AI in improving forecasting accuracy and operational efficiency, it largely treats AI as a standalone technological enhancement rather than as an integrated strategic capability embedded within broader governance and procedural systems.
A critical limitation in existing scholarship is the lack of empirical integration between structural governance mechanisms and AI-driven analytical capabilities within hybrid project contexts. Most prior studies examine either (1) traditional risk management practices without AI integration, or (2) AI-based analytics without embedding them within formal governance structures and aligned risk processes. There remains a significant absence of comprehensive structural models that simultaneously test how procedural alignment, governance consistency, operational visibility, escalation mechanisms, and AI-driven analytics interact to influence overall risk management effectiveness.
Moreover, the majority of empirical investigations into AI-enabled risk management rely on descriptive case studies, conceptual frameworks, or technology adoption surveys. Very few studies employ rigorous multivariate statistical approaches particularly Structural Equation Modelling (SEM) to examine causal relationships among governance structures, process alignment, AI capability, and performance outcomes. The absence of SEM-based validation limits the ability to understand not merely whether AI contributes to risk management, but how it structurally interacts with governance and process mechanisms to produce measurable effectiveness.
Hybrid project environments further intensify this gap. By design, hybrid methodologies integrate Agile adaptability with Waterfall predictability. While this integration offers flexibility and control, it also introduces structural fragmentation. Agile teams often operate with decentralized decision-making, iterative planning cycles, and lightweight documentation, whereas Waterfall components rely on formal stage-gates, structured risk registers, and hierarchical reporting channels. This methodological duality can create inconsistencies in risk documentation, ambiguity in escalation authority, duplication of monitoring mechanisms, and misalignment in governance oversight. Without deliberate structural harmonization, risk management processes may operate in parallel silos rather than as a coherent system.
Existing research has not sufficiently examined how AI capability functions within such fragmented environments. It remains unclear whether AI-driven analytics independently improves risk outcomes, or whether its effectiveness depends on aligned processes and consistent governance frameworks. Similarly, the relative contribution of structural factors (such as governance consistency and process alignment) versus operational mechanisms (such as visibility and escalation) has not been empirically disentangled in hybrid contexts.
Therefore, a significant research gap exists at the intersection of AI capability, strategic risk governance, and hybrid project environments. There is a need for an integrated empirical model that simultaneously examines multiple structural dimensions of risk governance and evaluates their direct influence on risk management effectiveness using robust statistical techniques. Specifically, the literature lacks:
Empirical SEM-based validation of AI-driven strategic risk management models in hybrid project settings.
Integrated structural testing of AI capability alongside governance consistency and risk process alignment.
Comparative assessment of structural versus operational risk mechanisms within hybrid environments.
Evidence clarifying how fragmentation inherent in hybrid methodologies influences risk governance effectiveness.
This study addresses these gaps by developing and empirically testing a comprehensive structural model of AI-driven strategic risk management within hybrid project environments. Using data collected from 384 professionals and analysed through Structural Equation Modelling (SEM), the study examines the direct influence of Risk Process Alignment, Risk Visibility Integration, Timely Risk Escalation, Governance Consistency, and AI-Driven Risk Analytics Capability on Risk Management Effectiveness. By integrating technological capability with structural governance dimensions, the research moves beyond isolated examinations of AI tools or traditional risk processes and provides a unified framework for understanding how hybrid risk governance systems can be optimized.
In doing so, the study contributes to advancing project risk management theory by positioning AI capability not as an auxiliary tool, but as a strategic enabler embedded within aligned procedural and governance architectures. It also responds to the increasing practical need for evidence-based guidance on managing risk in complex hybrid environments where methodological fragmentation and digital transformation converge.

CHAPTER-2
2. Literature Review
The effective management of risks is widely recognized as a critical determinant of project success, particularly in complex and dynamic environments where uncertainty, stakeholder demands, and regulatory requirements converge. Traditional project management approaches, such as the Waterfall methodology, emphasize structured planning and linear execution, providing predictability but often lacking flexibility to respond to emergent risks, while agile methodologies offer adaptability and iterative processes yet face limitations in large-scale infrastructure projects. Hybrid project management, which integrates the predictability of traditional approaches with the responsiveness of Agile, has emerged as a practical solution to address these challenges, particularly in infrastructure and large-scale projects. Concurrently, the advent of artificial intelligence (AI) has transformed risk management by enabling predictive analytics, real-time monitoring, and data-driven decision-making, enhancing the accuracy and effectiveness of risk mitigation strategies. 
This literature review critically examines the evolution of project risk management, the theoretical foundations of strategic risk governance, and the role of AI-enabled tools and methodologies in enhancing risk management effectiveness within hybrid project environments, highlighting key enablers, gaps, and opportunities for research-driven improvements.
Overview of Risk Management in Project Management
Project risk management (PRM) is essential for project success, focusing on identifying, analyzing, and mitigating risks throughout a project's lifecycle. It employs systematic approaches, integrating risk assessment and mitigation strategies, alongside ongoing monitoring to reduce uncertainties. According to (Okafor, 2021) effective PRM involves core principles, methodologies, and best practices, emphasizing the role of risk management tools, stakeholder engagement, and structured decision-making frameworks. Case studies highlighted in the research demonstrate that proactive risk management prevents cost overruns, schedule delays, and quality issues, stressing the importance of integrating risk management into project planning and execution to enhance resilience and sustainability.
(Adebayo, 2024) analyzed best practices in risk management, including risk identification, assessment techniques, and mitigation strategies. It emphasizes the importance of proactively seeking potential threats and prioritizing risks based on their impact. The article also discussed the evolving nature of project risks, exacerbated by technological advancements and global challenges like cybersecurity threats. A comprehensive table outlines key risk management tools, providing insights into effective applications across various sectors. Strong risk management not only protects project outcomes but also fosters organizational resilience, enabling adaptation to change and enhancing competitive advantage.
(Ebule, 2025) highlighted the increasing need for effective risk management in modern projects due to increasing complexity and uncertainty. It explores best practices in risk management and opportunity exploitation, focusing on risk definition, assessment, and management strategies within the context of project management. Key measures include stakeholder management and both quantitative and qualitative risk assessment methods. The discussion is bolstered by live examples, highlighting the role of effective risk management in facilitating positive change. 
(Abazid & Harb, 2018) Conceptualized risk and its consequences in construction and management operations. It discusses utilities and techniques for risk control in the construction industry, alongside the effect of risk on project assessment. The research focused on effective risk management implementation, evaluating various techniques to reduce risk, understanding the utility of risk management implementation, and identifying factors that influence risk management throughout project life cycles. Additionally, it distinguishes risk management practices specific to construction projects in the Arab region.
Strategic Risk Management: Theoretical Foundations
Strategic risk management (SRM) extends conventional risk management by integrating risk considerations into organizational strategy and governance frameworks. SRM emphasizes aligning risk policies with strategic objectives, promoting executive oversight, performance monitoring, and risk-informed decision-making. The theoretical foundations of SRM are derived from Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) theory, governance theory, dynamic capability theory, and the resource-based view (RBV). These frameworks collectively highlight the importance of proactive risk governance in sustaining organizational resilience and competitive advantage.
While SRM frameworks provide robust principles for governance and oversight, the literature suggests that empirical research on AI-enabled strategic governance in hybrid project contexts remains limited. Hybrid projects, which combine traditional planning with agile methodologies, present unique governance challenges that require integrating adaptive decision-making processes with predictive risk analytics.
Hybrid Project Management in Infrastructure Projects
Project management has historically relied on structured, sequential models, particularly the Waterfall approach, to manage large-scale infrastructure developments. These methods emphasize upfront planning, scope definition, detailed scheduling, and linear execution, offering predictability and control (Kerzner, 2023). In infrastructure projects where physical deliverables, fixed budgets, and regulatory scrutiny dominate such predictability is crucial. However, rigid models often fail to accommodate mid-project changes, emerging risks, or stakeholder demands, particularly in volatile economic or environmental conditions (Hosseini et al., 2023). 
The limitations of traditional methods in dynamic environments have fueled interest in more adaptive approaches. Agile project management, developed for fast paced software development environments, is characterized by short iteration cycles, frequent customer feedback, minimal upfront planning, and collaborative team structures (Hoda & Murugesan, 2023). Despite these advantages, the direct application of Agile in infrastructure is constrained by dependencies on physical assets, strict compliance requirements, and longer project durations (Khan & Al-Shammari, 2023).
Hybrid project management methodologies emerged as a practical response to the incompatibility of pure Agile in non-software environments. These approaches aim to combine the adaptability of Agile with the structure and control of traditional models (Praveen, 2025). In large-scale infrastructure settings, hybrid methodologies typically apply Agile principles in design, planning, or stakeholder communication phases, while maintaining traditional controls in procurement, construction, and regulatory compliance. Research by (Bjorvatn & Wald, 2023) emphasizes that hybrid approaches are particularly suited for projects operating in turbulent or uncertain conditions, where flexibility is needed but must be balanced with governance. Similarly, (Denicol et al., 2022) assert that the organizational architecture of megaprojects supports hybrid configurations, with core teams functioning iteratively while broader functions follow established protocols.
Traditional vs. AI-Driven Risk Management
Traditional risk management techniques and AI-driven risk management approaches significantly differ in their methods of data handling processes, analysis, and decision-making. In the traditional approach, risk management heavily depends on manual efforts to gather and scrutinize data from records and rely on insights from experts (Lundqvist, 2015). In contrast, AI-driven risk management exploits machine learning and sophisticated algorithm methods to effectively examine and evaluate immense volumes of data (Zigiene et al., 2019). AI systems can decipher complex data patterns, assess correlations, and forecast real-time potential hazards empowering organizations to react promptly and efficiently. Traditional approaches may struggle to scale and adapt to changing risk landscapes (Hopkin, 2018). Whereas AI-driven systems are highly scalable and adaptable. Traditional risk management relies on human judgments and familiarities, low initial investment, adaptability, and subjectivity; however, it may have limitations in scalability, response time, and predictive analysis. On the other hand, AI-driven risk management offers advanced analytics, predictive capabilities, automation, real-time insights, and a reduced potential for bias, but it may require greater technical expertise and raise concerns about data privacy and overreliance on technology (Schuett, 2023). Overall, AI-driven methods prioritize advanced analytics and automation, while traditional approaches are based on human judgment and familiarity. However, each of these approaches offers distinct advantages and complications in managing risks.
AI Techniques for Risk Management Practices
AI technologies have enhanced risk management practices by improving accuracy and efficiency in cost modelling, time forecasting, and risk modelling. These approaches allow organizations to leverage historical and real-time data to inform decisions, optimize resource allocation, and proactively mitigate risks.
Cost Modelling Methods
Cost modelling methods are compassed through a three-stage model comprising input, process, and output with a key aim of encompassing cost data, evaluation data, productivity, and safety data (Datta & Roy, 2010). To mitigate the volatility in project costs, a regularization neural network was formulated particularly for cost estimation purposes. An illustration of this is to forecast the costs associated with a reinforced concrete structural unit that is engineered to offer more stable and accurate cost estimation. Moreover, the utilization of artificial neural networks (ANNs) was carried out to monitor project productivity and safety (Matel et al., 2022).  A three-layered network containing a fuzzy output structure appeared to be most effective considering the subjective characteristics of a significant portion of the data. In order to improve the accuracy of scheduling analyses and risk identification, extensive AI databases have been established. These databases enable project managers to input new project tasks and instantaneously acquire feedback regarding predicted actual durations (Pan & Zhang, 2021). The verification process involved contrasting the AI output with real project data, demonstrating its validity, and offering invaluable information about potential project influencers for the intent of risk mitigation strategy. This method brings substantial time efficiency when compared to conventional spreadsheet approaches and advances the managerial ability for sustainable decision-making.
Time Modelling Methods
AI approaches for risk management processes contain a range of time modelling methods that attempt to predict the timing and duration of prospective risks accurately. These techniques deploy AI algorithms to evaluate past data, determine trends, and predict the timing of upcoming occurrences (Salehi & Burgueño, 2018). A time series analysis is a commonly employed methodology that entails the examination of sequential datasets in order to detect patterns, seasonal variations, and anomalies. Time modelling in risk management often involves the utilization of techniques such as autoregressive integrated moving averages (ARIMA) and seasonal decomposition of time series (STL) (He et al., 2022) (Schaffer et al., 2021). Furthermore, machine learning methods, such as recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and long short-term memory (LSTM) networks, possess the capability to effectively capture temporal relationships within datasets, enabling them to generate predictions pertaining to forthcoming time points (Sherstinsky, 2020). Top management can improve their capacities to anticipate and mitigate risks that occur over time by integrating time modelling methodologies into their risk management procedures, resulting in improved strategic planning and decision-making.
Risk Modelling methods
The application of artificial intelligence (AI) technology has had a profound impact on risk management practices, namely in the context of risk modelling procedures. These techniques utilize sophisticated algorithms and machine learning methods to analyse massive amounts of data, identify complex patterns, and forecast possible dangers with unprecedented accuracy in comparison to conventional methods (Belhadi et al., 2022). Prominent risk modelling techniques in the field of artificial intelligence encompass machine learning algorithms such as decision trees and neural networks, natural language processing for the analysis of textual data, and fuzzy logic for managing uncertainty (Bansal, M.; Goyal, A.; Choudhary, 2022). The integration of AI methodologies into risk management practices facilitates enhanced understanding, quantification, and the mitigation of risks in various industries, including finance, insurance, cybersecurity, and project management. In turn, this integration fosters more informed decision-making processes and yields improved outcomes in risk management.
AI-Driven Risk Management for Sustainable Decision-Making
The existing literature on decision-making identified different enablers that are raising organizations’ concerns for sustainable decision-making practices (Isensee et al., 2021) (Yigitcanlar, 2021) (Abid et al., 2021) (Javaid et al., 2022). In the context of AI, as suggested by (Yaseen, 2021), leveraging AI for proactive risk identification, organizations can analyse vast datasets to pinpoint potential risks across various operational facets, including environmental impact, supply chain resilience, and regulatory compliance. Moreover, AI-driven risk assessments offer a comprehensive approach, considering environmental, social, and economic factors, facilitating holistic evaluations and the prioritization of actions to minimize adverse effects on sustainability objectives (Abid et al., 2021) . AI-enabled data driven decision-making assists businesses to act on insights from past data and prediction models, ensuring that actions are in accordance with sustainability objectives and risk-free. In addition to maximizing sustainability impact while minimizing waste and optimizing overall performance, AI improves resource allocation by spotting inefficiencies and redirecting resources accordingly. By keeping checks on data in real-time, organizations are able to adjust to unfamiliar circumstances rapidly, availing full advantage of sustainability potential while mitigating risks (Ahmed et al., 2022). Therefore, risk management enabled by AI not only aids sustainability initiatives but also facilitates organizations’ survival and thriving in a constantly changing world.

Hypothesis Development
Impact of Risk Process Alignment on Risk Management Effectiveness in Hybrid Project Environments
Risk process alignment refers to the consistent integration and synchronization of risk management activities such as risk identification, analysis, response planning, and monitoring across both Agile and Waterfall methodologies within hybrid project environments. Hybrid methodologies combine the structured planning of Waterfall with the iterative flexibility of Agile to address complex project requirements. Research shows that hybrid risk management enhances resilience and delivers superior project outcomes by blending proactive risk identification with adaptive responses, provided that risk processes are aligned across methodologies (Leech, 2025). 
Moreover, evidence suggests that misalignment between sequential planning and iterative delivery often leads to inconsistent risk practices, resulting in duplication, oversight gaps, and reactive risk responses that undermine project objectives (Ibrahim & Bezverkhyi, 2024). Empirical studies also point out that hybrid frameworks achieve higher risk responsiveness and stakeholder satisfaction when methodological boundaries are bridged through aligned risk processes (Praveen, 2025). 
Additionally, the literature on hybrid risk management emphasizes that alignment of governance mechanisms and risk workflows across project phases improves visibility, reduces escalation delays, and enhances decision quality (Székely et al., 2025). Collectively, these studies indicate that when risk process alignment between Agile and Waterfall is achieved, hybrid projects are better positioned to handle uncertainty and improve risk mitigation effectiveness. Based on the reviewed literature, the following hypothesis is proposed:
H1: Risk process alignment has a significant positive impact on risk management effectiveness in hybrid project environments.
Influence of Risk Visibility Integration on Risk Management Effectiveness
Risk visibility integration denotes the practice of consolidating risk information from both Agile and Waterfall components into unified dashboards, reports, and communication channels so that all stakeholders have access to up-to-date risk status and analytics. Research increasingly highlights that higher risk visibility enhances decision-making efficiency in complex projects by enabling early identification of threats and facilitating coordinated mitigation across teams. For instance, risk visibility allows cross-functional teams to detect emerging risks before they escalate, creating opportunities for rapid alignment of mitigation actions (Jacob, 2025).
Literature on hybrid project governance emphasizes that real-time risk tracking and integrated reporting systems improve collaboration between agile teams and formal planners, thus reducing ambiguity and information silos that typically impair hybrid risk management effectiveness (Mirzaei et al., 2025). Furthermore, studies show that enhanced visibility of risk metrics across project stages increases transparency, fosters accountability, and supports informed strategic interventions.
Finally, research on automated risk analytics and dashboards argues that tools which integrate risk signals from different methodologies are associated with improved responsiveness and reduced negative project outcomes, particularly in hybrid contexts where disparate teams coexist (Nwosu & Peter, 2025). Therefore, the following hypothesis has been developed: 
H2: Risk visibility integration has a significant positive impact on risk management effectiveness in hybrid project environments.
Effect of Timely Risk Escalation on Risk Management Effectiveness
Timely risk escalation refers to the rapid elevation of critical risks to decision-making authorities or governance bodies once certain thresholds (e.g., cost impact, schedule variance, regulatory exposure) are breached. In project environments where Agile iterations and Waterfall milestones coexist, the delay in escalation often results in response lag, reduced mitigation window, and weakened control over strategic risks. Recent studies show that structured escalation protocols tied to predefined risk triggers significantly improve oversight and proactive governance, enabling leadership to intervene before risks cause cascading disruptions (Jacob, 2025). 
Research on risk governance in hybrid settings further supports that escalation thresholds and formal notification mechanisms improve alignment between cross-team processes, ensuring that high-impact risks receive priority intervention (Székely et al., 2025). Comparative analyses demonstrate that projects with timely escalation systems outperform those with ad-hoc escalation practices across metrics such as schedule adherence and stakeholder satisfaction. 
Moreover, real-time risk management literature argues that escalation mechanisms embedded within automated analytics alert systems enable more rapid responses to risk deviations, resulting in improved resilience and reduced negative outcomes.  Based on the reviewed literature, the following hypothesis is proposed:
H3: Timely risk escalation has a significant positive impact on risk management effectiveness in hybrid project environments.
Role of Governance Consistency in Enhancing Risk Management Effectiveness
Governance consistency refers to the uniform application of policies, standards, roles, and decision rights across the hybrid project lifecycle. Consistent governance reduces ambiguity, ensures adherence to risk thresholds, and aligns risk oversight across methodological boundaries. Recent systematic research on hybrid risk governance emphasizes that standardized governance frameworks improve accountability, risk ownership, and integration of cross-team processes (Ibrahim & Bezverkhyi, 2024). 
Additionally, literature on project risk and governance highlights that when governance structures incorporate both predictive and adaptive risk controls, risk responses are implemented more rapidly and consistently, reducing variance in outcomes across teams (Nguyen, 2022). Empirical evidence also suggests that consistent governance intersects with organizational culture and leadership support to strengthen risk management effectiveness, particularly in environments with multiple methodologies. 
Finally, research on digital risk management frameworks shows that governance consistency ensures that automated systems and manual processes adhere to shared risk policies, leading to fewer gaps and more cohesive mitigation strategies (Kapri, 2023). Based on the reviewed literature, the following hypothesis is proposed:
H4: Governance consistency has a significant positive impact on risk management effectiveness in hybrid project environments.
Impact of AI-Driven Risk Analytics Capability on Risk Management Effectiveness
AI-driven risk analytics capability refers to the use of advanced technologies such as machine learning, predictive modeling, and real-time data analytics to enhance risk detection, forecasting, and decision support within project environments. Research consistently highlights that AI integration improves risk identification accuracy, enables continuous monitoring, and supports proactive risk mitigation far beyond traditional techniques that rely on manual judgment and static data (Peri, 2025). 
Recent literature further emphasizes that AI systems significantly enhance project risk forecasting accuracy through predictive patterns and continuous learning, enabling managers to anticipate disruptions and adjust plans accordingly. Systematic reviews of AI risk methods show that hybrid AI models combining multiple analytical techniques provide robust insights into complex risk interdependencies and support better risk prioritization and resource allocation in uncertain environments (Tanim & Ahmad, 2025). 
Additionally, research suggests that AI-augmented risk management not only increases detection capacity but also supports governance practices by providing automated dashboards, alerts, and evidence-based recommendations, which improve strategic decision-making (Tian, Kun, 2025).  Based on the reviewed literature, the following hypothesis is proposed:
H5: AI-driven risk analytics capability has a significant positive impact on risk management effectiveness in hybrid project environments.
Research Gap
Despite the extensive literature on project risk management (PRM) and strategic risk management (SRM), several gaps remain in understanding how these practices function within hybrid project environments that combine Agile and Waterfall methodologies. Existing studies predominantly focus on traditional risk management approaches or general AI applications in project risk contexts, with limited empirical evidence on how AI-driven risk analytics integrates with hybrid risk governance to enhance effectiveness. While prior research has highlighted the benefits of risk process alignment, visibility integration, timely escalation, and governance consistency, most studies have examined these factors in isolation, often within either fully Agile or fully Waterfall projects. Furthermore, although AI techniques such as neural networks, machine learning algorithms, and predictive analytics have been explored for cost, time, and risk modelling, there is a paucity of research investigating their practical implementation and impact on decision-making across hybrid project phases. The literature also indicates limited attention to how AI-enabled risk management supports sustainable decision-making and organizational resilience in dynamic and complex project environments. Therefore, a comprehensive empirical investigation that integrates AI-driven capabilities with hybrid risk management practices addressing alignment, visibility, escalation, and governance is needed to fill these gaps and provide actionable insights for enhancing risk management effectiveness in hybrid projects.

A quantitative research approach was considered most suitable for this study because the primary objective is to examine and statistically test the impact of AI-driven strategic risk management on risk management effectiveness across hybrid project environments. Variables
Risk Process Alignment
Risk process alignment refers to the degree to which risk management processes are consistently structured and integrated across different project methodologies (such as Agile and Waterfall). It involves aligning risk identification, assessment, response planning, implementation, and monitoring processes so they work coherently across hybrid project environments, enabling smoother coordination and decision-making.
Risk Visibility Integration
Risk visibility integration is the extent to which risk information is consolidated, shared, and accessible across stakeholders and project layers, enabling continuous awareness of risk exposures and facilitating timely decision-making. It includes transparent documentation, dashboards, and communication channels that integrate risk data between teams, tools, and governance structures (Schön et al., 2020). 
Timely Risk Escalation
Timely risk escalation refers to the prompt reporting and escalation of risk events or triggers to appropriate decision-making levels when risks exceed predefined thresholds or require strategic intervention. This ensures that critical risks are addressed before they evolve into significant issues impacting objectives.
Governance Consistency
Governance consistency denotes the uniform application of risk governance structures, policies, roles, and decision-rights across project teams and organizational units. It includes consistent oversight mechanisms, reporting lines, and accountability for risk activities to ensure strategic alignment and compliance with organizational risk frameworks.
AI-Driven Risk Analytics Capability 
AI-driven risk analytics capability refers to the organizational ability to apply artificial intelligence, machine learning, and advanced analytics methods to detect, analyse, predict, and visualize risk patterns from structured and unstructured data. This capability enhances predictive insight into future risks, automates risk detection, supports prioritization, and provides actionable recommendations.
Risk Management Effectiveness in Hybrid Projects
Risk management effectiveness in hybrid projects refers to the degree to which risk management practices successfully achieve desired outcomes minimizing negative impacts on project objectives (scope, cost, time, quality) while enabling opportunities through systematic identification, evaluation, response, monitoring, and control of risks within hybrid project environments (combined Agile and Waterfall).
